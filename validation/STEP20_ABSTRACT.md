# WaterChain — registration abstract

Field teams investigating drinking-water contamination work from spreadsheets,
where a single positive lab result is easily mistaken for proof of origin,
failed QC goes unnoticed, and unrelated batches merge into one story. The
costly failure mode is the **unsupported conclusion** — remediation directed
by a pathway the evidence never supported.

WaterChain is a local-first investigation workspace that makes unsupported
conclusions harder to reach. Investigators register sources, sampling events,
water/container entities, and documented transfers, then run a transparent
rule-based engine that either names a candidate contamination-transition
interval or explicitly **abstains with a named reason**. A 15+-rule
eligibility gate, lineage and chronology checks, and wording constraints (no
probabilities, no causal claims) guard every inference; per-record
eligibility, unresolved-evidence explanations, a tamper-evident audit ledger,
and traceable follow-up actions make each decision reviewable by a second
person. It runs offline on commodity hardware.

On 13 adversarial synthetic fixtures, the engine made zero unsupported
attributions where the documented naive baseline made them in 8 of 9 cases —
a fixture-conformance result, not field accuracy; independent review and user
evaluation are prepared and pending. WaterChain contributes to **SDG 6**
(clean water and sanitation) by strengthening the evidence discipline behind
contamination investigations, helping scarce remediation effort follow
traceable evidence rather than the loudest positive result.
